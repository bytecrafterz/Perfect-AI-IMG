------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 0. THE DIRECTION IN ONE PARAGRAPH
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

She opens the app from her phone's home screen. She taps one button and picks
a photo. Style options appear, chosen by the robot for that photo. She marks
what she wants - several per row if she likes - and taps once. Six options
appear one by one within half a minute. She taps the keepers, and they arrive
finished.

One button, a few taps, under two minutes. No prompts, no menus to learn, no
settings to understand, no password, no reviewing for defects, no retrying.

The infrastructure remains free: a permanently-free cloud machine, automatic
HTTPS, no monthly bill. Money is spent only when she generates, under caps she
sets, at roughly $0.07 per finished photo.

 1. WHAT A WEBSITE COSTS, AND WHAT IT WINS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

This has to be said plainly, because it is the substance of the change.

WHAT TELEGRAM WAS GIVING AWAY, THAT MUST NOW BE BUILT
-----------------------------------------------------
  Mobile UI on iPhone and Android      -> build it, responsive, touch-first
  Photo picking and upload             -> build it, with retry on bad signal
  Authentication                       -> build it (see below - it is easy)
  Push notification when work finishes  -> build it, with an iOS caveat
  Permanent gallery of everything made  -> build it
  Progress feedback during a wait       -> build it (SSE - and it comes out
                                           better than Telegram's)
  Hosting of the client-facing surface  -> your machine now serves it

  Honest estimate: +2 to +3 days. Not the six to eight weeks a full product
  frontend would cost, because this flow is genuinely simple - an upload
  button, seven rows of chips, two image grids and a gallery. There is no
  dashboard, no settings maze, no design system to invent.

WHAT THE WEBSITE WINS - AND SOME OF IT IS SIGNIFICANT
-----------------------------------------------------
  1. THE UPLOAD PROBLEM DISAPPEARS
     In the Telegram design she had to remember to send photos as "Archivo"
     and not as "Foto", or they arrived compressed and unusable. That was a
     listed HIGH risk, mitigated only by detecting the mistake afterwards.
     A web upload sends the original file every time. The instruction is
     gone, the risk is gone, and for someone who told us plainly that file
     handling is her difficulty, tapping one button and picking from her
     camera roll is easier than anything Telegram could offer.

  2. PROGRESSIVE RESULTS
     Telegram albums arrive all at once, so six previews mean 25 seconds of
     nothing followed by everything. On the web each preview appears the
     moment it finishes. She watches the set fill in. The same wait feels
     half as long, and it makes the robot look like it is working.

  3. THE UI THIS FLOW ACTUALLY WANTS
     Seven multi-select chip rows with a live combination counter is clumsy
     as chat messages and natural as a web screen. Same for tap-to-select on
     an image grid, pinch-to-zoom on a result, and a before/after comparison
     slider - which was the one thing revision 3 could not do well.

  4. IT IS HERS
     Her own address, her own name on it, her own gallery. When she has
     clients of her own it is already a product rather than a bot she has to
     explain. The phase-2 multi-tenant product is this codebase, not a
     rewrite.

  5. NO PLATFORM DEPENDENCY
     No third party's policy changes can affect the delivery of her work.

WHAT IS GENUINELY WORSE
-----------------------
  1. She must open it once from a link and add it to her home screen. That is
     a one-time, guided, two-minute step - and it needs hand-holding, because
     she is not technical. Section 3 has the exact wording to send her.
  2. Push notifications on iPhone only work once the app is on her home
     screen. Section 6 covers this properly, including the fallback.
  3. It is more code, so there is more that can break, and it must be kept
     running. Telegram never went down on our account.

THE RECOMMENDATION
------------------
Build the website. The upload win alone justifies it for this particular
client, the flow suits a screen better than a chat, and it is the phase-2
product built early rather than thrown away. But go in knowing it is +2 to +3
days against a fixed budget, and cut Stage 6 items to phase 2 to pay for it.


------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
 2. WHAT IT IS, TECHNICALLY - A PWA
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Not a native app. Not an app-store submission. A normal website, written so
that a phone treats it like an app:

  ADD TO HOME SCREEN     an icon on her phone, opens full screen, no browser
                         chrome, no address bar. Indistinguishable from an app
                         to a non-technical user
  NO APP STORE           no USD 99/year, no review, no update cycle. You
                         deploy, she reloads
  NATIVE PHOTO PICKER    the phone's own camera-roll UI, one tap
  WEB PUSH               background notification when a job finishes
  OFFLINE SHELL          the app opens and shows her gallery even on a bad
                         connection; only generation needs the network
  ONE CODEBASE           iPhone, Android, and her laptop if she ever wants a
                         big screen

AUTHENTICATION - THE SIMPLEST THING THAT IS SAFE
------------------------------------------------
No password. No email verification loop. No "create an account".

  You send her one private link, once. She opens it. A signed, long-lived
  cookie is set on her phone and she is permanently logged in on that device.
  Optionally a 4-digit PIN on open, if she wants her phone protected.

  For a single-user private tool this is both the least friction possible and
  entirely adequate. Multi-user auth arrives in phase 2, when there is
  actually more than one user.

MULTI-SELECT IS THE MECHANISM - THREE STATES PER ROW
----------------------------------------------------
Every attribute row accepts many selections, and how many she picks is what
tells the robot what to do. One uniform rule:

    NOTHING selected   ->  the robot varies it freely across the 6
    ONE selected       ->  fixed. Identical in all 6
    SEVERAL selected   ->  varies across EXACTLY those values, nothing else

  She is not choosing one style - she is describing a space, and the previews
  are drawn from inside it. "The dress and the casual outfit, standing and
  walking, smiling softly" is four taps, and it is a photo shoot brief.

  On the request it is one field, uniform across every attribute:

    "selections": {
      "garment":    ["vestido", "casual"],
      "gesture":    ["de pie", "caminando"],
      "expression": ["sonrisa suave"],
      "hair":       ["el mio"],
      "scene":      [],                      <- empty = robot varies it
      "framing":    ["medio"]
    }

HOW THE SIX COVER HER SELECTION
  combination space = cartesian product of the selected rows

    space <= 6   cover EVERY combination, then use leftover slots to vary a
                 row she left empty
    space >  6   sample evenly across the space - never six variations of one
                 corner - keeping a cursor so [ Otras 6 ] continues the walk
                 instead of repeating it

  Selecting more raises variety, not price. The number of previews is what
  costs money, and it does not change.

  Previews drawn from what she actually asked for get kept more often than
  ones the robot guessed at, so multi-select should also lower cost per
  finished photo. Real effect, unmeasured until the POC - do not put a number
  on it for her yet.

GESTURE CHIPS ARE ORDERED BY RELIABILITY
  Hands are where generators fail most, and the gate already measures it.
  Sort the GESTO chips by their own first-try rate, so reliable poses sit
  first and fragile ones sit last. She never sees the mechanism; she just
  finds the obvious choices work.


THE OTHER WAYS TO SET STYLE - ALL TAP OR VOICE, NEVER COMPOSITION
-----------------------------------------------------------------
  A. CHIP ROWS - the everyday method, shown above.

  B. REFERENCE PHOTO - she uploads a photo she is not in. The app detects
     that automatically and asks what to take from it:
       [ La ropa ] [ El peinado ] [ La pose ] [ La luz ] [ El sitio ]
     The garment is read - cut, fabric, colour, length, neckline - and
     becomes one more chip in the ROPA row, so it combines with everything
     else. Same upload button, two meanings, resolved by the robot.
     This is the method she will reach for most.

  C. HER OWN WARDROBE - garments photographed once become named, reusable
     chips: ( Mi vestido negro ). Upload once, tap forever.

  D. REFINE FROM A RESULT - any finished image offers [ Mas como esta ] and
     [ Igual pero otra ropa / otro sitio / otra pose / otra expresion ].
     One axis changes, everything else holds, seeded from an image she has
     already approved. Highest hit rate in the product.

  E. VOICE - a microphone button on the style screen. She says it out loud,
     it is transcribed, turned into chip selections, and shown back marked so
     she sees what was understood before anything is generated. For someone
     who dislikes technical interfaces, speaking beats typing. Typed text
     also works but stays out of the interface.


SAFE DEFAULTS - LOCKED UNLESS SHE ASKS
--------------------------------------
  Face, body proportions and skin tone are ALWAYS locked and are not offered
  as options in the POC.

  Hair defaults to HER hair and is never a variation axis. Changing it is a
  deliberate tap, because hair strongly affects whether an image reads as her.

  Body adjustment: the brief asked for natural aesthetic adjustment; she later
  complained a tool had slimmed her unasked. One rule satisfies both - the
  proportion check blocks ANY unrequested body change and relaxes only for
  the axis she explicitly requested, by the amount requested. Never automatic,
  never silent. Deferred to phase 2 regardless.

 4. THE LOOK CATALOG AND THE PROPOSAL ENGINE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Unchanged from revision 3.1. The catalog is where your expertise is stored so
she never has to supply any, and it is matched against the photo she just
uploaded rather than browsed.

A LOOK IS A STRUCTURED RECIPE, NOT A TEXT PROMPT
------------------------------------------------

  {
    "id": "moda_terraza_atardecer",
    "name": "Terraza al atardecer",
    "category": "moda",
    "cover_image": "covers/terraza.jpg",

    "recipe": {
      "garment":  { "type": "vestido largo", "fabric": "satinado",
                    "colors": ["negro", "verde oscuro", "burdeos"] },
      "scene":    { "place": "terraza urbana", "time": "hora dorada",
                    "depth": "ciudad desenfocada al fondo" },
      "lighting": { "key": "sol bajo lateral", "fill": "rebote suave" },
      "camera":   { "focal_mm": 85, "aperture": "f/2.0",
                    "height": "pecho", "framing": "plano medio" },
      "pose_family": ["de pie apoyada", "caminando", "girando"]
    },

    "applies_to": {
      "framing":     ["medio", "cuerpo entero"],
      "needs_body":  true,
      "replaces_background": true,
      "min_source_quality": 0.6
    },

    "variation_axes": ["gesture", "camera_angle", "garment_color", "expression"],
    "locks": ["face", "body_proportions", "skin_tone", "hair"],
    "selectable": ["garment", "hair", "gesture", "expression",
                   "scene", "light", "framing"],
    "chips": {
      "garment":    ["vestido", "traje", "casual", "deportivo", "abrigo"],
      "hair":       ["el mio", "suelto", "recogido", "coleta", "ondas"],
      "gesture":    ["de pie", "caminando", "sentada", "apoyada", "girando",
                     "manos en la cintura", "mano en el pelo"],
      "expression": ["neutra", "sonrisa suave", "sonrisa amplia", "seria",
                     "mirada baja", "riendo"],
      "scene":      ["como esta", "exterior", "estudio", "ciudad", "playa"],
      "light":      ["como esta", "natural", "dorada", "estudio", "dramatica"],
      "framing":    ["como esta", "cuerpo entero", "medio", "primer plano"]
    },
    "chip_stats": { },

    "route_hint": "in_place_edit",
    "learned_params": { },
    "stats": { "first_try_rate": null, "avg_cost": null, "keep_rate": null }
  }

  applies_to makes contextual proposal possible - it rules out a full-body
  scene when she uploaded a head-and-shoulders photo.

  selectable and chips define the multi-select rows. chip_stats accumulates
  per-chip first-try and keep rates, which orders the GESTO row by
  reliability and lets dead chips be retired.

  A QUICK STYLE IS JUST A SAVED SELECTION. Tapping [ Editorial moda ] fills
  every row at once and jumps straight to previews. One mechanism underneath,
  with shortcuts on top - not two systems to build.

THE PROPOSAL ENGINE - RULES AND STATISTICS, NO TRAINING
-------------------------------------------------------
  score = applicability(photo, look.applies_to)      hard filter
        + w1 * look.stats.keep_rate                  her taste
        + w2 * freshness(look, recent_sessions)      keeps the grid alive
        + w3 * look.stats.first_try_rate             favours what works

  Top 4-6 become the quick-style tiles; the rest sit behind "Ver todos".

COVERS BECOME HERS
------------------
Tiles ship with covers you generate. After the first sessions, replace each
cover with her own best kept result in that style. The grid then shows her
face in every option - far more persuasive than generic examples, and free,
because the images already exist.

WHO FILLS THE CATALOG
---------------------
You do, once. Budget a full day for 15-20 looks. Manual professional work,
and it is what she is actually buying - weak recipes make a weak product no
matter how good the pipeline is.

  Moda 5-6 - Retrato 3-4 - Escenarios 3-4 - Producto 2-3 - Estacional 2-3

The catalog is data, not code. Adding a look is one JSON file.

IMAGE DELIVERY - A NEW CONCERN THAT TELEGRAM HANDLED FOR US
-----------------------------------------------------------
Full-resolution photos over mobile data are slow and expensive for her.

  Grids       WebP thumbnails, ~40 KB each, generated once on arrival
  Full screen medium WebP, ~200 KB
  Download    original full-resolution file, only when she asks
  Caching     immutable URLs with long cache headers; Caddy serves them
              directly without touching the app

  Without this the gallery is unusable on a phone. It is half a day of work
  and it is not optional.


UPLOAD RELIABILITY - THE OTHER THING TELEGRAM DID FOR FREE
----------------------------------------------------------
  Client-side resize before upload only if the file is enormous - never
  below the quality floor the profile needs. Retry with backoff on a dropped
  connection. Show real upload progress. Accept HEIC and convert server-side
  with pillow-heif. Never silently drop a photo.


PREVIEW FIDELITY - THE ONE REAL RISK, AND HOW IT IS HANDLED
-----------------------------------------------------------
If a chosen preview does not become the final she expected, the whole design
collapses. Two ways to guarantee it, decided on day 3 with real measurement:

  PREFERRED   img2img from the accepted preview at low denoise, plus upscale.
              The final is provably derived from the image she chose, so it
              cannot drift. Slight ceiling on maximum quality.

  ALTERNATIVE same seed and parameters re-rendered at full resolution.
              Higher quality ceiling, but seed behaviour across resolutions
              varies by provider and must be measured, not assumed.

  Decision rule: measure drift on ~20 pairs. Below the agreed perceptual
  threshold, use the alternative; otherwise the preferred. Either way she
  gets what she picked.

  If a final fails the gate outright it is regenerated silently. If it fails
  repeatedly it is dropped and she is told plainly which one and why - the
  only place in the product where she sees a failure, and it should be rare.


THE FOUR CONTRACTS
------------------
No module anywhere references a provider by name:

  LookRecipe          applies_to, variation_axes, locks, selectable, chips
  AttributeIR         her identity and the scene; locks and mutable
  QAReport            verdict, scores, defects with bounding boxes, action
  ProviderDescriptor  capabilities, resolution, cost, latency, quality priors

Adding a provider = one ~150-line adapter plus a descriptor. Adding a look =
one JSON file. Neither touches the pipeline.


LEARNING - WHAT IT REALLY IS
----------------------------
With one user there is no data to train a model, and saying otherwise would
be selling air. Three mechanisms, working from about the tenth session:

  1. PARAMETER MEMORY  parameters behind chosen previews get promoted into
                       that look's learned_params and seed the next session
  2. PROVIDER BANDIT   Thompson sampling per (look, provider), rewarded on
                       chosen-per-euro, not merely on passing the gate
  3. THRESHOLD FITTING where her choices disagree with the gate's ranking,
                       the gate is wrong. Fit the cutoffs to her, not to a
                       paper

Every session produces both positive and negative labels for free: six shown,
three chosen, three not. That is real preference data at zero effort from her.

BACKEND LANGUAGE   Python 3.11

    manifest.json + icons           add-to-home-screen

  WHY NOT REACT OR NEXT.JS
  This UI is an upload button, seven chip rows, two image grids and a
  gallery. Alpine handles that in a few hundred lines with no build
  pipeline, no Node in the container, and one deployable artefact. A React
  frontend would add a second toolchain and a build step to a one-week
  project without improving anything the user can see.

  Phase 2, when this becomes a branded multi-tenant product with client
  accounts, is the right moment for Next.js - and the Python API underneath
  does not change when that happens.

  Progressive enhancement matters here: the chip rows must work as plain
  form controls even if JavaScript is slow to boot on her phone.

 10. RISKS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  RISK   Preview does not faithfully predict the final
  IMPACT HIGH - the central technical risk
  FIX    Validated day 4 on ~20 measured pairs, before Stage 6 depends on it.
         Preferred method makes drift structurally impossible. Fallback is
         single-stage at ~$0.10.

  RISK   She never completes "add to home screen"
  IMPACT HIGH - without it there is no icon and no iOS push, and she may
         simply not come back
  FIX    Do it WITH her on a call, on her phone, once. Do not send
         instructions and hope. The exact wording is in section 3, but the
         walkthrough is what actually works. It is also the last item in
         Stage 8 for a reason.

  RISK   Web push unreliable on her device
  IMPACT MEDIUM
  FIX    Push is a convenience, not load-bearing - waits are 25 and 60
         seconds and the SSE connection covers an open page. Results always
         wait in the gallery. Optional Telegram notification channel as
         belt-and-braces.

  RISK   Website adds days the budget does not have
  IMPACT HIGH
  FIX    Named explicitly: wardrobe, voice and standing orders move to phase
         2. Say this in writing before starting, not when the week runs out.

  RISK   Gallery unusable on mobile data
  IMPACT MEDIUM
  FIX    WebP derivatives and cache headers from day one, not as an
         optimisation later. Half a day, in Stage 3.

  RISK   Style tiles feel irrelevant to the photo she uploaded
  IMPACT HIGH - the proposal is the "it thinks" moment
  FIX    applies_to filters hard before ranking. Test across her real photo
         types on day 2. Always offer "Ver todos".

  RISK   The catalog is too narrow and she gets bored
  IMPACT HIGH
  FIX    15-20 looks at launch, not 6. Rotate seasonal looks. Watch keep_rate
         and retire dead ones. [ Mas como esta ] gives infinite depth inside
         a style she already likes.

  RISK   The gate is the only approver and lets something bad through
  IMPACT HIGH - no human safety net
  FIX    Stage 5 calibration on her own photos, held-out validation set,
         conservative thresholds at launch. Better to over-reject early - it
         costs money, not trust.

  RISK   Latency budget missed; two minutes becomes six
  IMPACT MEDIUM - it changes the feel of the product completely
  FIX    Concurrency from the start, not as an optimisation. Progressive SSE
         delivery hides part of the wait. Cheap preview tier is
         non-negotiable.

  RISK   The site goes down and nobody notices
  IMPACT MEDIUM - new with self-hosting; Telegram never had this
  FIX    A free uptime monitor pinging /health, alerting you. Docker restart
         policy. Nightly SQLite backup off the box.

  RISK   Received photos lack full-body coverage
  IMPACT MEDIUM - limits full-scene styles and weakens the proportion
         baseline that catches slimming
  FIX    Assessed day 1, reported immediately. One targeted ask if short.

  RISK   ARM wheel availability for a CV dependency
  IMPACT MEDIUM
  FIX    Mitigated by the no-PyTorch constraint. Verify day 0.

  RISK   Consent and sensitive content
  IMPACT HIGH - legal
  FIX    Consent record from day 1, invisible watermark on outputs, source
         material deleted when the POC closes as promised. Lingerie deferred
         until the consent procedure is reviewed - already agreed.
         Note: a public URL raises the stakes over a private chat. Auth on
         every image route, unguessable filenames, no directory listing.

  RISK   Provider price change or deprecation
  IMPACT LOW
  FIX    ProviderDescriptor holds pricing; the ledger reads from it. A price
         update is a config change, not a code change.

      with no instruction about file types
